# Ninety-day pilot contract

All example inputs are fictional. A generated plan is a **draft for human review**, not approval to connect an account or automate an action.

## Days 1–30: find a bounded problem

Name one owner, one task, and its source. Record at least one paired manual baseline sample with total minutes and an accepted-output standard. The example uses eight invented baseline samples summarized by a 75-minute median. A real pilot should record each actual task and its source rather than copying that figure.

## Days 31–60: connect and validate under supervision

The first pilot uses an approved read-only source and local draft output. Require a human reviewer. Pair each assisted task with the baseline method and record operator, review, correction, and fallback time. The fictional target is at least 9 accepted outputs in 10 tasks and median total assisted time at most 60 minutes. These are exercise thresholds, not universal standards.

## Days 61–90: decide from the complete record

Stop on a missing source date, contradictory decision, or unapproved action request. Recheck reliability, authority, false alarms, maintenance cost, and any outcome the team actually measured. The Chapter 14 fixture has a negative illustrative projection and `NOT_MEASURED` delivery improvement. It cannot justify expansion. A human should decide whether to continue a bounded pilot, change it, or retire it after reviewing real evidence.

Calendar ranges are inclusive: days 1–30 start at `start_date`, days 31–60 start 30 days later, and days 61–90 start 60 days later. The program validates the charter fields and emits all three phase dates even when it finds a blocker. It does not attest that a task, permission, or review has actually occurred.
