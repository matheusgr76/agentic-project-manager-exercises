# Chapter 2 — initial assessment and after-action review

**Compare your work:** The [CSV packet](source-records.csv) separates initial Records A–H, variation I, and after-action Records J–N. Read the [worked example](example-output.md) after drafting your own response.

**Tools:** a document editor and a chatbot. Allow about 30–45 minutes for the initial assessment.

## Initial assessment

1. Read [Records A–H](../../acme-practice-packet/records-chapters-1-3.md#chapter-2--initial-project-assessment). Write your own sections for objective, confirmed facts, unknowns, dependencies, and required decisions. Select two or three priorities before Friday's review.
2. Ask the assistant for an assessment with source references, separated facts and assumptions, tentative decision owners, parallel work, three immediate priorities, and a management briefing of at most 180 words. Do not let it invent a baseline, delivery date, approved scope, or available capacity.
3. Compare its output with your assessment. It should recognize Alpha's effect on Bravo's estimate, Charlie's preliminary test work, provisional capacity, and unfinished prior-release closure.
4. Add Record I, the Thursday Teams approval claim, without removing Record C. Ask for a revised assessment. The new message does not identify the approved version, approver, or Bravo's acknowledgement.
5. Write your own Friday response to the CEO: known position, decisions still needed, and the next defensible commitment. Improve wording with the assistant only after making that judgment.

## Separate after-action review

Read [Records J–N](../../acme-practice-packet/records-chapters-1-3.md#chapter-2--separate-after-action-review). First list observations you can support and possible causes that need confirmation. Then ask the assistant for cited observations, candidate process improvements, a small pilot, a measurement plan, and three questions for team members. Do not claim time savings or root causes that the records cannot establish.

## Check the result

The assessment must support an honest management conversation without a fabricated delivery date. The after-action review should preserve the customer-specific communication condition and distinguish a repeated report from proof of wasted hours. A useful pilot is one checked fact sheet feeding two audience drafts, measured over two cycles including review time and corrections.

Save the packet, your assessment, prompts, both assistant versions, CEO response, and after-action review.
